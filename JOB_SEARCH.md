## Recommendations — updated 2026-07-01

### Resume tweaks
1. **Quantify impact.** Add numbers where you can defend them: TVL/notional secured by your contracts, latency of the WebSocket indexer, number of audit findings triaged, throughput of the matching engine. Recruiters and senior engineers skim for numbers first.
2. **Lead with the AI-agent angle, don't bury it.** Your summary line already does this well ("Lately I've been using AI agents to automate on-chain execution") — keep it, and consider pulling the ERC-8004/8183 agent marketplace bullet up higher in the Nunchi block since "agentic on-chain infra" is one of the hottest, least-saturated niches in web3 hiring right now.
3. **Publish audit reputation.** You have real audit-lifecycle experience (Quantstamp/Zellic coordination) but no public track record (Code4rena/Sherlock/Cantina). A couple of confirmed medium/high findings in public contests is the single strongest signal for security-engineer roles and is worth more than another line on the resume.

### If companies keep going AI-first — lean into what you already started
You're ahead of most Solidity engineers here — most job descriptions for "AI-first" web3 roles just want someone who (a) uses AI coding agents fluently and (b) understands agent-to-agent/agent-to-chain protocols. Concretely:
- **Use Claude Code / Cursor with an MCP server wired to Slither + Foundry** as your default dev loop, not just for the side project — being able to talk about *daily* AI-in-the-workflow usage in interviews (not just a demo project) is what "AI-first" companies are actually screening for.
- **Deepen ERC-8004 / x402 / agent-payment-protocol knowledge.** This is the intersection of your two skill sets (AI agents + EVM) and very few candidates have shipped anything real here. Your Nunchi agent marketplace work is a strong story — turn it into a blog post or conference talk proposal to build inbound.
- **Formal verification** (Certora, Halmos) is the next tier up from Slither-based auditing and increasingly requested for senior/security roles — a natural next skill given your audit background.

### If any skills feel dated
- Nothing here is outdated, but **Rust/Anchor is your weakest-maintained skill relative to your EVM depth** — if you want SVM roles as a fallback, ship one more recent Solana/Anchor project; otherwise it's fine to keep as "secondary" and not invest further, since your primary target (EVM) doesn't need it.
- Move is listed but you have no described project using it — either add a short bullet on where you used it, or consider dropping it if it's untested; an unsubstantiated skill on a resume this strong invites a question you can't answer well.
